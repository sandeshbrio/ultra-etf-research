Sentiment Drivers

  1. Alphabet's miss is the morning's engine (bearish tech). Revenue $127.6B vs $128.2B expected, adjusted EPS $2.67 vs $3.02 — its first double miss in nearly four years — with search ads ($82B vs $83.6B expected) blamed on AI Mode monetizing worse per query, and free cash flow at −$9.07B, the second straight negative quarter, as capex hit $51.4B in the quarter and the FY guide stayed $195–205B with spending set to rise "significantly" in 2027. Shares fell ~10–11% premarket, wiping nearly $400B off its market value. The market's new grading rule is visible: cloud doubling (+103% to $30.7B) did not save a cash-burning balance sheet. Reuters via WNCY, CNBC via suaragarut, The Indeks
  2. Microsoft's beat is the offset (bullish quality). Revenue $92.3B vs $90.7B expected, adjusted EPS $4.89 vs $4.73, Azure +48% to $33.2B with 49–50% growth guided this quarter, and capex below what the market feared — with free cash flow still positive at $18.6B. Shares rose ~3–4% after hours and hold +0.7% premarket. The split verdict — Microsoft rewarded, Alphabet punished — says AI spending is fine only while cash flow covers it, and that is the lens for Amazon and Intel tonight. The Indeks, Finnhub (MSFT)
  3. The Fed hold is priced; December is the live question (hawkish undercurrent). The 10–2 hold with two hawkish dissents plus Warsh's "monetary medicine is starting to work" tone left December-hike odds at ~83–85% and the 10-year back above 5.02%. Today's 8:30 AM PCE is now the December-hike referendum: a hot core print makes the hike near-certain; a soft one gives the market room to doubt it. Reuters via WDSM, TalkMarkets
  4. Samsung's record-but-silent quarter cools the memory trade (watch SOXL). Record results on memory demand, yet shares fell 4–5.4% because management said nothing about boosting returns — the mirror of SK Hynix's +4.6% on its return plan. Combined with Alphabet's capex scrutiny, the chip complex enters the US session with its two-day euphoria dented, which is why SOXL's −6.8% premarket move is bigger than the Nasdaq's. Reuters via WNCY
  5. Oil eased on peace-talk hopes, but the stagflation chain is still armed (watch, don't chase). Brent back toward $110 and WTI ~$101.5 after the reported Vance letter to Tehran, unwinding part of Wednesday's refinery-attack spike. A hot PCE print today would re-arm the oil-plus-hikes narrative into Friday's Employment Cost Index. TipRanks, TalkMarkets
