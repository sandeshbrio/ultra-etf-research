Sentiment Drivers

  1. The Commerce Department closed the rental loophole — Monday's selloff had a policy trigger, not just nerves. A new rule requires licenses before advanced AI chips go to data centers in five countries, aimed at Chinese firms renting computing power abroad. It landed the same day CNBC reported Nvidia's board is not discussing Jensen Huang's return — the petition for his return has passed 180,000 signatures — and NVDA fell 4.1% to a 52-week low, with Micron and Intel down 7%+. The rule gives the chip rout a fundamental leg, not just a sentiment one, and it frames tonight's print: the call under interim CEO Kress will be pressed on China exposure. World Money Roadmap, Markets in Plain English
  2. Warsh's first address landed dovish on rates, hawkish on the AI story. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh spoke Monday — his first scheduled public address since the hot CPI — setting out three scenarios for superintelligence and warning AI could disappoint. The market heard the AI caution more than any rate signal: the 10-year barely moved (5.121%), but chips sold off through the session. With a December hike priced at ~100%, Warsh did nothing to soften that pricing. Objective Daily News evening watch, Markets in Plain English
  3. Asia sold chips for a second straight day — the de-risking is pre-emptive, not reactive. The KOSPI's fourth straight loss, the Taiex's second 2%+ drop in two days, and the Nikkei's second straight decline are investors cutting AI exposure ahead of Nvidia's print, not reacting to it. US futures firming into the open while Asia sells is the same pattern as Monday's brief — and Monday's futures firmness did not survive the cash session. MLQ, HK Businesswire
  4. Main Street beat, Wall Street shrugged. Home Depot (revenue $43.4B, +4.9%; adj. EPS $3.86; comps +2.2%) and Disney (revenue $25.95B, +16%; adj. EPS $1.77 vs ~$1.67 expected; dividend +33%, $3B cost-savings target) both beat before the open — and neither moved the tape. HD is down ~2% on its CEO transition (finance chief Richard McPhail takes over Nov 23); DIS is roughly flat. On a day owned by retail sales and Nvidia, two beats that can't move futures are the tell: the consumer is fine, the market doesn't care — it wants the chip print. Samuel & Co open brief, Longbridge
  5. The Nasdaq is in a correction with the VIX above 22 — and options price an 8.5% Nvidia move either way. The Composite is more than 10% below its October 6 high; the VIX closed at 22.91 (+18.7%) and sits ~22.6 premarket. Nvidia's options imply ±8.5% on the print — roughly a $13 move on a $156 stock. A 3x chip ETF can gap 25% on that. The setup is binary in both directions, and the tape knows it: breadth was 1,933 up vs 7,651 down Monday, and the premarket bounce is narrow (Nasdaq firm, Dow and Russell down). World Money Roadmap, StockSetups
