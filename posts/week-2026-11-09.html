  1. Broad market — a CPI-reaction week, not a trend week (TQQQ/SQQQ, UPRO/SPXU). Tuesday's 8:30am CPI and Friday's 8:30am PPI are the two binary windows; everything else is positioning or aftershock. TQQQ at $52.25 after a −15.7% week and below its 200-day average is a bounce vehicle, not a reclaim — the long side needs QQQ to retake $680.89 (the 200-day) before it earns conviction, and SQQQ at $47.70 after a +14.0% week is extended enough that a soft CPI print could squeeze it hard. Trade the reaction to each print, never the print itself, and be flat into both opens.

  2. Semiconductors — the whipsaw pair, now priced for whipsaw (SOXL/SOXS). SOXL −40.3% on the week with a −38.7% Thursday and a +12.5% Friday; SOXS +36.2% with a +39.2% Thursday and a −12.6% Friday. Both sides just proved they can move a third of a historic gain in one session. The week's chip catalysts are direct: CoreWeave Tue AMC, Applied Materials and Cisco Thu AMC. If CoreWeave validates AI-infrastructure demand, SOXL's Tuesday/Wednesday tape is a squeeze candidate off the $46 level; if it guides soft, SOXS continuation has a second leg. Size for a 20%+ daily range in either name — this week proved that range is real, twice over.

  3. Small caps — the rotation vehicle broke (TNA/TZA). Russell −2.05% week, TNA −7.0% despite Friday's +2.8%: the money leaving mega-cap tech is no longer landing here — Thursday took small caps down with everything else, and the 10-year at 4.86% with a December hike at ~78% is the headwind that breaks the small-cap bid. And with no market-moving earnings after Friday's close, the book can be flat into the weekend without an after-hours catalyst hanging over it.

  4. CoreWeave Tuesday night is the week's first single-stock gap risk (TECL/TECS, SOXL/SOXS). The AI-infrastructure bellwether reports after the close Tuesday, hours after the CPI print — two binaries stacked in one day. A clean CoreWeave print would give the chip tape its first genuine demand-side validation since the rout; a soft one would confirm the market is grading AI names on cash flow, not growth, and extend the unwind into Wednesday's thin Veterans Day session. SOXL/SOXS carry this risk directly; TQQQ/SQQQ carry it with better liquidity — prefer them.

  5. Rates + oil — the two switches that override earnings. The 10-year at 4.86% after a 7bp weekly decline is the cushion that kept Friday from being worse; a hot Tuesday core CPI pushing it back toward 5% re-opens the tech drawdown. Brent at $98.32 and WTI at $90.83 after a ~13% weekly collapse is the other switch — energy was the week's worst sector despite the Hormuz headlines, so the oil tape is now a demand signal, not a geopolitical one, and a further slide would confirm the slowdown fear behind the equity rout. Gold at ~$4,075 (+4.5% week) finally acted like a hedge — NUGT is tradeable on its own levels now, not as a tech-headline proxy.
