Sentiment Drivers

  1. The Nvidia probe is the morning's shock — and the tape is so far containing it. Nvidia's board announced Sunday (Nov 8) an independent investigation, overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel, into reports that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. The inquiry follows a New York Times report (Oct 29) that US intelligence concluded Alice Huang — a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer — was a Chinese intelligence asset, and that she had a close relationship with Huang. Huang acknowledged in a message to employees that he was unfaithful to his wife and apologized; the board said it has "no reason to believe" US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and Huang remains CEO and is cooperating. Three House committee chairmen have requested a closed-door briefing with Huang on Nov 18. Shares are −4.9 to −5.8% premarket (~$175.50) — yet Dow and S&P futures are up ~1% and ~0.6%: the market is treating this as a single-name governance shock, not a new AI thesis. The risk is contagion: if the probe widens or the Nov 18 briefing turns adversarial, the chip tape re-opens Thursday's wound — and Nvidia reports earnings Nov 17, nine days away. The Hindu BusinessLine, EE News Europe, Reuters via 985 The River
  2. Asia's chip rebound is real, but it is policy-assisted — and Seoul already faded it. The Taiex's +4.9% came with Taiwan's regulator cutting short-sale caps and readying a NT$500B stabilization fund; the KOSPI gave up a +1.5% morning gain to close −0.8% on foreign selling. The rebound is a short-covering and dip-buying move, not a fundamentals reset: the AI-capex and regulatory fears that drove Thursday's rout are unresolved, and US-listed memory and cloud names are falling premarket (CoreWeave −3.53%, IREN −3.79%, Nebius −2.72%, Oracle −0.98%). SOXL's modest +2.3% premarket — against a +4.9% Taiex — says the US chip tape is not yet convinced. Saxo Market Quick Take, MLQ
  3. Extreme Fear is the backdrop, and the crowd is calmer than the institutions. CNN Fear & Greed closed Friday at 22 (Extreme Fear), with four of seven components in Extreme Fear; the Adanos retail tracker reads 40 (Fear) — a −18-point smart-minus-retail gap, at or beyond its ±17-point band. Contrarian logic says this setup precedes sharp relief rallies — but Tuesday's CPI is a genuine binary that can deepen the fear first, and December-hike odds near 80% after Friday's hot jobs print (+165K payrolls vs ~65K expected) are the headwind that has to break before a bounce becomes a trend. Week-2026-11-09 brief, DailyForex
  4. Oil's slide flips the energy signal from geopolitical to demand. WTI −2.93% to $88.17 and Brent ~$97.32 after Trump said further strikes on Iran were unlikely — on top of a ~13% weekly collapse last week, when energy was the worst sector (XLE −12.00%) despite the Hormuz headlines. The oil tape is now a demand signal, not a geopolitical one, and a further slide would confirm the slowdown fear behind the equity rout. For the watchlist it means no clean energy hedge today; gold (+1.22% this morning) is the cleaner haven read. Swingfolio morning, DSIJ pre-market

Trading Lens

  • Monday is a positioning day, not a setup day — size for the CPI print that comes tomorrow, not for today's bounce. The week brief's house rule stands: no reason to carry size through a positioning day into a binary. The bounce is real (futures +0.5–1.0%, Asia's chip rebound, VIX easing to ~23.5) but narrow, fading in Seoul, and contradicted by US-listed semis falling premarket. Trade the reaction to Tuesday's 8:30 AM CPI, never the print itself, and be flat into it by rule.
  • SOXL/SOXS: do not chase the gap in either direction — the two tapes disagree. Asia says chips are rebounding (+4.9% Taiex, TSMC +5.6%); US premarket says they are not (Micron −1.26%, SanDisk −2.35%, CoreWeave −3.53%, Nvidia −5%). SOXL at +2.29% premarket is the compromise price. Either way, last week's lesson holds: this pair moved 38.7% in a day and gave back a third of it before the bell — size down.
  • TQQQ/SQQQ: the Nasdaq is still in correction, and Nvidia is its largest single-name risk. TQQQ +2.09% premarket after −15.7% on the week, with QQQ still below its 200-day ($680.89). SQQQ has the edge only if the cash session fails to hold the premarket highs while Nvidia keeps sliding; TQQQ only becomes the trade if the session holds above Friday's range and the Nvidia shock stays contained. Watch QQQ $660 (the call wall / flip line) in the first 15 minutes.
  • TNA is the morning's breadth tell — and FAS the Dow's. TNA +3.53% premarket is the strongest watchlist move and the only one with a breadth story behind it; if small caps hold the lead through the open, the bounce is broadening past chips and UPRO/TNA become the cleanest continuation trades. If TNA fades and the Dow's +0.95% futures lead evaporates, the bounce is a one-day short-cover and the inverse side (TZA/SPXU) re-engages fast. And with no market-moving earnings after the close, the book can be flat into the evening without an after-hours catalyst hanging over it — the same discipline that kept it clean through the rout.
