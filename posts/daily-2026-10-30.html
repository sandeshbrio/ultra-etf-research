Sentiment Drivers

  1. Amazon's miss completes the hyperscaler verdict (bearish tech). Q3 revenue $201.94B (+12%) narrowly missed ~$202.2–202.3B expected, EPS $1.89 vs ~$1.96–1.97; AWS grew 39% to $46B — its fastest in nearly five years — yet shares fell ~7% premarket on a soft holiday-quarter outlook (Q4 revenue guide $240–247B vs ~$244.5B expected) and a reaffirmed ~$220B annual capex plan that drove trailing free cash flow to a −$42.4B outflow. The market's grading rule is now three-for-three: Microsoft (cash-flow positive) rewarded, Alphabet and Amazon (cash-burning) punished. Market Briefer, InvestingLive, Superpower Daily
  2. Intel's guide cut is the chip sector's own shock (bearish SOXL). Q3 revenue $16.5B (+21% y/y) beat, but Q4 guidance of $15–16B revenue and $0.25 adjusted EPS fell far short of ~$17.1B and $0.43 expected; the CFO warned the PC slowdown lasts at least through H1 2027, and a factory explosion in Israel cut ~$300M from the outlook. Shares fell ~21–26% premarket — the direct cause of SOXL's −20% indication and Asia's chip rout (Samsung, SK Hynix, Advantest, Tokyo Electron all down hard). Market Briefer, SSBCrack
  3. The AI-spending question has flipped from "how much growth" to "who pays" (structural). Three straight days of punishment for cash-burning AI spenders — Meta last week, Alphabet Thursday (−12.64%, ~$500B value wiped), Amazon and Intel overnight — while the Dow holds flat and small caps rose Thursday. The tape is rotating out of AI capex, not out of equities. That rotation is what keeps UPRO/TNA viable even as TQQQ/SOXL crater. Market Briefer, Pulsafi
  4. Yields falling is the cushion, not the catalyst (watch the 10-year). The 10-year at a seven-week low (~4.89–4.94%) is a flight-to-safety move, not a dovish repricing — December-hike odds are still ~75–85% after Wednesday's hawkish-dissent hold. A hot ECI print at 8:30 AM would push yields back up and re-arm the "hikes plus oil" stagflation chain into the weekend. TradingView, Lead-Lag
  5. Oil above $100 with energy earnings this morning (watch FAS, not just tech). Brent ~$116 and WTI ~$103 on Middle East tensions, with ExxonMobil and Chevron reporting before the open — the one sector where today's catalyst is genuinely positive. A strong XOM/CVX print plus firm oil argues for FAS resilience even in a tech rout. TipRanks, Admiral Markets

Trading Lens

  • SOXL/SOXS: a −20%/+24% gap is a decision, not a trade signal. Do not chase SOXS at the first print — gaps this large routinely retrace. The trade is continuation only if SOXL keeps sliding below its premarket lows after the open, or a reversal if it reclaims ~$90–100 on a soft ECI. Either way, size small: a gap this wide means stops are wide too.
  • TQQQ/SQQQ: same rule, smaller gap. SQQQ +12% premarket has momentum, but the 8:30 AM ECI print lands minutes after this brief and can unwind part of it. Wait for the print's first 15 minutes to set direction, then trade the continuation or the reversal — never the gap itself.
  • UPRO/SPXU and TNA/TZA: the rotation vehicles. The Dow flat and small caps' Thursday resilience mean the broad market is a two-way tape even in a tech rout. These are the cleanest vehicles for trading the ECI reaction itself, since they carry no single-stock earnings risk — and TNA is the candidate if the ECI comes in soft and the rotation back into risk resumes.
  • FAS: the one positive catalyst this morning. XOM/CVX report into $103 WTI. A strong oil-major print argues for FAS resilience independent of tech; a weak one removes the tape's last bid. Check the prints before leaning either way — and remember FAZ only with tight spreads confirmed.
