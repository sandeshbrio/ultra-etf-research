Sentiment Drivers

  1. Qualcomm and Arm broke the AMD reprieve. Qualcomm beat on EPS ($2.17 vs ~$2.16) but missed revenue ($10.44B vs ~$10.48B) and guided soft (Q1 EPS ~$2.25 midpoint vs ~$2.27 expected); handset revenue — its largest business — fell 26% to $5.14B, and at least eight brokerages cut price targets. Shares −9 to −10.1% premarket. Arm beat (revenue $1.44B, +27% YoY; EPS $0.52 vs ~$0.48) but guided cautiously ($1.54B ± $50M vs ~$1.52B expected) and fell ~2% after hours after closing −5.28% Wednesday. The read: phone chips are soft, AI-infrastructure exposure is what gets paid — and Qualcomm has less of it than the market hoped. After AMD was rewarded for its beat 24 hours earlier, the grading curve just snapped back. SiliconANGLE, SRN News/Reuters, The Retest
  2. The rotation is the story, not a retreat. Dow futures +0.6%, Russell +0.7%, Hang Seng +3.7% — while Nasdaq futures and every chip-heavy Asian market fall. This is money moving within equities, from the AI chain into value, small caps, miners and Chinese internet. For leveraged day-trading that means the index-level signal and the sector signal now point opposite ways: UPRO/TNA can work while TQQQ/SOXL bleed, and vice versa if the rotation reverses. Trade the leg you are in, not the index you wish you were in.
  3. Oil's crash removed a volatility leg — and gold took its place. WTI −8.3% Wednesday to $88.12 on Strait of Hormuz reopening hopes; Brent ~$94. The energy gap-risk that haunted Monday/Tuesday is gone for now, but gold's +1.5% overnight move to ~$4,150 on a softer dollar is the new haven flow. Watch whether gold's strength is a dollar move (NUGT tradeable) or a fear move (broader risk-off, which would hit TQQQ harder).
  4. ADP leaned hot into Friday's payrolls. Wednesday's ADP printed 86K vs ~69K expected — firmer hiring than the rate-cut case assumed, and it keeps the December-hike case alive (CME FedWatch ~71% for a hike at Dec 8–9 as of Oct 30). Today's claims (197K expected vs 194K prior) and Q3 productivity are the last labor reads before Friday's jobs report; a hot pair re-arms the hike case and undercuts the rally — the setup SPXU/TZA would feed on. Bank of Vol, The Retest
  5. The Bank of England hikes into a global rates split. A 25 bp BoE hike to 4.00% is ~98% priced — the first since August 2023 — while the Fed sits at 3.75–4.00% after hiking in September and the market still prices a December Fed hike. UK 10-year gilts near 5.16% will take their cue from Bailey's press conference at 7:30 AM ET; a hawkish tone can spill into US yields and the leveraged tape.

Trading Lens

  • SOXL/SOXS: do not catch the knife, do not chase the gap. SOXL is down ~20% in two sessions ($73.12 → $58.69 premarket) — the worst air pocket in the watchlist's most liquid vehicle. A long continuation needs SOXL reclaiming Wednesday's $66.68 close in the first 15 minutes; a short continuation needs a failed bounce under ~$62. Either way, size down: a 3x ETF moving 12% premarket can move another 12% by noon. SOXS after a +12% gap is a fade candidate only on a confirmed SOXL reclaim, not a chase.
  • TQQQ/SQQQ: the Nasdaq is still in correction — Wednesday was a bounce, not a reclaim. TQQQ −2.60% premarket gives back most of Wednesday's +2.79%. SQQQ has the morning's edge while Nasdaq futures stay red; TQQQ only becomes the trade if the cash session holds above Wednesday's range through the 8:30 AM claims print.
  • UPRO/TNA: the rotation's long vehicles. Dow futures +0.6% and Russell +0.7% are the morning's strongest legs. If claims come in soft and the rotation holds, UPRO and TNA are the cleanest continuation trades — no single-stock earnings risk, and breadth is on their side. A hot claims print flips both to SPXU/TZA fast.
  • NUGT/DUST and FAS: secondary expressions, sized small. Gold's +1.5% gives NUGT a genuine tailwind, but it is a dollar move — trade it as its own thesis. Financials ride the Dow leg and the ~4.89% 10-year; FAS only on the claims reaction, not on the gap. Be flat before Cloudflare/Atlassian after the close — the same rule that kept the book clean through AMD, Qualcomm and Arm applies again.
