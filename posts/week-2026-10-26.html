5. Trading Implications (day-trading lens)

  1. Broad market — the FOMC + PCE week is a reaction-trading week (TQQQ/SQQQ, UPRO/SPXU). Wednesday 2:00pm decision + 2:30pm Warsh presser, then MSFT/GOOGL after the close, then Thursday 8:30am GDP + PCE: three consecutive event windows, each capable of a 1%+ index swing. TQQQ sits on its 200-day (~$65.21) with the S&P below its 50-day — the long side needs a reclaim of the S&P 50-day (~$7,699.64) to work; until then SQQQ/SPXU keep the momentum edge intraday, but size them for reversal — Extreme Fear weeks produce the sharpest short squeezes on any dovish surprise.

  2. Semiconductors — faded, not fixed (SOXL/SOXS). SOXL +1.8% Friday after −20.5% Thursday is stabilization, not a turn: chips are down three straight weeks and the SOX is −3.2% on the week. The week's chip catalysts are second-order (MSFT/GOOGL capex commentary Wed night, INTC/SNDK Thu night, SK Hynix Tue night) rather than a direct SOX earnings slate — so SOXL/SOXS will trade off hyperscaler capex language. SOXS fading 1.9% Friday from Thursday's highs is the first sign the crowded short is tiring; a clean MSFT/GOOGL capex print makes Thursday's open a SOXS squeeze candidate.

  3. Small caps — the rotation vehicle, and the cleanest trend (TNA/TZA). Russell +2.0% week, TNA +5.6% on the week, equal-weight S&P +1.13%: the money leaving mega-cap tech is landing here, helped by the 10-year back below 5%. TNA is the week's cleanest momentum long into Tuesday's confidence/durable-goods data — but oil above $100 and a hot Thursday PCE are the two things that break the small-cap bid, so TZA is the flip on either trigger, not a hold-through.

  4. Gold miners + energy — oil is the trade, gold is not (NUGT/DUST, FAS). Brent $116.72 (+6.2% week) is the market's live tail risk; gold below $4,000 (−4.1% week) proves miners are not the hedge this cycle. Don't chase NUGT on geopolitical headlines — the working energy expression this week is Friday's XOM/CVX earnings and the oil tape itself, and FAS follows Visa (Tue AMC) and Mastercard (Thu BMO) card-spending reads, not the oil move.

  5. Rates + dollar — the cushion trade. The 10-year below 5% (4.988%) after Thursday's flight-to-safety rally is what lets beaten-down tech stabilize at all; a Thursday PCE beat that re-prices December-hike odds above ~90% would push yields back through 5% and re-open the tech drawdown. Watch the 10-year's 5.00% level as the session's risk switch alongside the VIX 20 handle.
