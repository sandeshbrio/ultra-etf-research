Sentiment Drivers

  1. Oil pullback = the morning's relief valve (bullish). Brent down ~2% from Friday's near-$117 high (highest since 2022) on reports of Qatari mediation efforts and hopes of de-escalation with Iran — the direct unwind of Friday's Dow −652 selloff. If Brent holds below ~$115 into the open, the growth scare eases; a re-spike re-arms the whole risk-off chain. Reuters via wncy, SP Angel
  2. The AI-monetization test is the week's frame (neutral-to-bearish for tech). "Strong AI demand alone may no longer be enough. Markets want evidence of monetization, margins and cash flow," per Saxo's Charu Chanana — the through-line from Meta's and Tesla's muted receptions last week into Microsoft/Alphabet (Wed AMC) and Amazon (Thu AMC). Neoclouds remain the bleeding edge: CoreWeave and Nebius each −3% premarket, down 30%+ this month; Musk said Sunday that markets have "almost entirely shut off access to money" for firms without revenue independent of AI data centers and predicted neocloud consolidation. Reuters via wncy
  3. Extreme Fear, but stable — and pros are not capitulating (contrarian bullish undertone). CNN Fear & Greed closed Friday at 21 (Extreme Fear), second straight session in the zone; AAII bears 45.8% vs bulls 36.9% (−8.9% spread). But the NAAIM exposure index rose to 87.7, its highest since late September — professionals are still heavily invested even as retail fear deepens. That's the setup that historically precedes sharp relief rallies — but Wednesday's FOMC + hyperscaler double-header can deepen the fear first.
  4. Verizon's beat is a quiet positive for the AI-infrastructure adjacency (bullish telecoms). Q3 adjusted EPS $1.31 on $34.3B revenue (revenue slightly light vs $34.74B expected), but postpaid phone adds of 406,000 nearly doubled the 209,439 expected, FY EPS guidance raised to $5.04–$5.06, and a >$2B AI-infrastructure deal with AWS signed (after July's >$1B Google dark-fiber deal). Shares +2.4% premarket — a reminder that AI capex is creating revenue streams outside the hyperscalers themselves. Reuters via ktwb
  5. Central-bank week positioning (neutral — the real event is Wednesday). Fed decision Wed 2:00 PM (hold at 3.75–4.00% expected, ~90%+ priced; Bank of America the outlier expecting a hike), Warsh press conference 2:30 PM; ECB Thu, BoJ Fri. Today is positioning, not decision — the FOMC blackout ends with the statement, and no Fed speakers are on today's calendar. NordFX Market Pulse, IC Markets

Trading Lens

  • SOXL/SOXS: gap-and-hold is the whole trade. SOXL +4.8% premarket after Friday's +1.8% stabilization is the first real bounce attempt since Thursday's −20.5% capitulation. The week guide's read holds: SOXS fading from Thursday's highs is the crowded short tiring. If the gap holds through the first 30 minutes, SOXS is the squeeze candidate; if it fades back under $84, the downtrend reasserts and SOXS re-opens. Don't pre-commit — let the open decide.
  • TQQQ/SQQQ: relief bounce, not a trend change. TQQQ +2.0% premarket sits on its 200-day (~$65.21) with the S&P still below its 50-day (~$7,699.64). Extreme Fear (21) weeks produce the sharpest short squeezes on any dovish surprise — SQQQ is the momentum side per the week guide but the crowded side, and a quiet Monday relief tape is exactly when shorts bleed. Size for reversal either way.
  • TNA/TZA and FAS: wait for the data. No tier-one data today means small caps and financials trade off the tape, not their own catalysts. TNA is the week's cleanest momentum long (Russell +2.0% last week, TNA +5.6%) — but with oil above $100 and the FOMC two days out, the flip to TZA on an oil re-spike or a hot Chicago Fed read is the discipline, not a hold-through.
  • NUGT/DUST: still unusable as a hedge. Gold back above $4,000 is a toe over a round number after a −4.1% week, not a haven bid — the commodity move is all oil. Treat miners as risk-off collateral damage; the energy-inflation channel trades through the oil tape, not the miner ETFs.
  • Positioning day, not decision day. Today's job is to watch whether the oil pullback and the SOXL/TQQQ gaps hold into the close — that sets the entry levels for Tuesday's durable-goods/confidence data and Wednesday's FOMC.
