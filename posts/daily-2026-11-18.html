Sentiment Drivers

  1. Nvidia beat the week's peak binary — and the reaction is relief, not euphoria. Fiscal Q3 revenue $115.84B (+103% YoY) vs ~$109B expected; adjusted EPS $2.68 vs $2.47; data center $107.6B (+110%). Q4 guided to $130B ±3% vs ~$125B expected, assuming no data center compute sales to China. Interim CEO Kress raised the FY2028 revenue-growth outlook to ~75% (from 70%) and added $150B to the buyback. The stock jumped as much as 4% after hours, finished after-hours +4.8% at ~$162.95, and sits +3.1% premarket — a solid but restrained response given the scale of the beat, with the options-implied ±8.5% move not fully delivered. Vera Rubin did ~$20B in its first production quarter, the fastest ramp in company history. Market Unclouded, FXStreet, Swingfolio
  2. The leadership question is settled for now — the investigation is not. Board chairman Harvey Jones said the directors unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and have no intention of reinstating him, despite a petition passing 180,000 signatures. The WSJ reported the SEC is investigating Nvidia, and the earnings release gave no update on the probe. Kress passed her first leadership test by guiding at all; restoring the valuation premium will take longer than restoring confidence in the business. Cointelegraph, FXStreet
  3. Tuesday's data kept the December hike fully priced — and the minutes are today's second binary. October retail sales rose +0.2% m/m, in line, with the core (control group) +0.6% vs +0.4% expected; import prices rose +1.1% vs +0.7% expected — hot. Industrial production fell 0.4%. CME FedWatch priced a ~95% chance of a quarter-point December hike on Tuesday, effectively 100% in some tallies. The 2:00 PM FOMC minutes (Oct 27–28 meeting, 10–2 vote to hold at 3.75–4.00%) are the afternoon's event, with a 20-year auction at 1:00 PM and Fed speakers Barr (1:35 PM), Williams (2:30 PM) and Kashkari (4:15 PM) around it. eOption, MarketThread, Swingfolio
  4. Retail earnings beat — and got sold anyway. Target beat (adj. EPS $2.65 vs $2.07 expected, comps +5.6%, raised full-year sales outlook) and fell 2.4%; Lowe's beat (rev $22.8B vs $22.4B, adj. EPS $3.10 vs $2.89, including a $0.34 tariff refund) and fell 4.3% after cutting its operating-margin outlook to ~11.5%. Same pattern as Home Depot and Disney on Tuesday: beats that can't move the tape. The market is trading the Nvidia print and the Fed, not the consumer. Swingfolio, First Feed News
  5. Gold broke down while stocks bounced — the hedge is not hedging. December gold fell below $3,800 for the first time in over a year (−2.38% to $3,772.30), silver hit a 52-week low, and the dollar sits near a 19-month high (DXY 103.81). With the 10-year pinned above 5.1% and a December hike fully priced, the rate trade is crowding out the safety trade — DUST, not NUGT, is the gold pair with momentum this morning. Swingfolio
