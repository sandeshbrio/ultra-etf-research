Sentiment Drivers

  1. The midterm sweep is now the AI story. The market's read on the Democratic sweep flipped twice in three days — gridlock relief on Wednesday, AI-spending slowdown fear on Thursday — and Thursday's version won by a landslide: Nasdaq −5.5%, semis −12.3%, $2.5T erased. The fear is that a Democratic Congress slows AI infrastructure build-out and tightens AI curbs, and it spread overnight to every chip-heavy market in Asia. This is a political driver, not an earnings one, so it can reverse on a headline as fast as it arrived — exactly the whipsaw risk the algo's rules are built to avoid chasing. CNN, TipRanks
  2. Cloudflare proves the panic is not universal — and the market is buying that distinction. Cloudflare beat big (Q3 revenue $782.5M, +39% YoY vs ~$737M expected; EPS $0.38 vs ~$0.34) and raised full-year guidance (Q4 revenue $845–846M vs ~$798M expected) — shares +14–19% premarket. Along with memory names rebounding (Micron +8.3%, SanDisk +8–10%), this is the market separating AI infrastructure demand from AI capex financing fear. The bounce is narrow and name-specific, which is why the index futures are split: Nasdaq +1.4%, Dow −0.8%. InvestorsHub/ADVFN, Frenzy Capital
  3. The jobs report is the day's binary — and the December hike is on the line. Consensus is payrolls +65K (range ~35K–131K) vs +29K in September, unemployment 4.2%, average hourly earnings +0.3% m/m (2.9% y/y, the first sub-3% print since May 2021). Markets price a ~65–80% chance of a December Fed hike. A hot print (or strong wages) re-arms the hike case and can re-ignite Thursday's selloff — the setup SOXS/SQQQ would feed on. A weak print cuts hike odds and validates the bounce. Leading indicators lean firm: ADP +86K vs ~69K expected, JOLTS openings up to 7.523M, Challenger cuts at their lowest since 2023. FXStreet, The Retest, TradingKey
  4. VIX +70% says this was institutional de-risking, not noise. The VIX's jump to 28.18 was its largest intraday surge in over a year, and VIXY (the VIX futures ETF) gained ~30% Thursday. Credit spreads, notably, tightened 8 bp to 332 bp — the selloff is sentiment- and flow-driven, not a credit-market breakdown, which leaves room for a fast reversal if the jobs print cooperates. Daily Market Report, Finnhub (VIXY)
  5. Oil is rebounding while gold falls — the haven map is scrambled. Brent back near $99 (+1.6%) and WTI ~$91–92 on tanker attacks and uncertain US–Iran talks, while gold fell ~1% to ~$4,045–4,082. The usual risk-off pattern (gold up, oil down) is inverted: energy is a geopolitical move, gold is being sold with everything else. For the watchlist that means NUGT/DUST have no clean haven signal today — trade them only on their own levels, not as a proxy for the tape.

Trading Lens

  • SOXL/SOXS: do not chase a ±17% premarket gap in either direction. SOXL +16.75% premarket after −38.71% Thursday, SOXS −16.82% after +39.22% — these are the largest two-day swings in the watchlist's history, and a 3x vehicle can give back a gap like this before 10:00 AM. A long continuation needs SOXL reclaiming Thursday's range in the first 15 minutes after the 8:30 AM jobs print; a short continuation needs the bounce to fail under it. Either way, size down: yesterday's rule-8 winner paid +10.26% on reduced size precisely because the entry waited for the driver to be live and worsening, not priced in.
  • The 8:30 AM jobs print is the trade — everything before it is noise. Futures are bouncing into the print, not through it. A hot print (payrolls well above 65K, wages above +0.3%) re-arms the December hike and re-ignites the selloff: SOXS/SQQQ become the trade on a failed bounce. A weak print cuts hike odds and validates the bounce: SOXL/TQQQ become the continuation. Be flat-or-positioned around 8:30, not mid-trade.
  • TQQQ/SQQQ: the Nasdaq is in correction — the bounce is a test, not a reclaim. TQQQ +3.82% premarket after −18.92% Thursday and −30.3% over five days. SQQQ has the edge only if the cash session fails to hold the premarket highs through the jobs print; TQQQ only becomes the trade if the session holds above Thursday's range and the print cooperates.
  • UPRO/TNA: the rotation's long vehicles are the weakest legs this morning. Dow futures −0.8% and Russell +0.12% say the bounce is chips-only. If the jobs print is soft and the bounce broadens, UPRO and TNA are the cleanest continuation trades — no single-stock earnings risk, breadth on their side. A hot print flips both to SPXU/TZA fast. And with no market-moving earnings after the close, the book can be flat into the weekend without an after-hours catalyst hanging over it — the same discipline that kept it clean through AMD, Qualcomm, Arm and Cloudflare.
