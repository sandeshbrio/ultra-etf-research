Sentiment Drivers

  1. Apple beat — and fell anyway (the day's main lesson). Fiscal Q4 revenue $114.48B (vs ~$113B expected), EPS $2.10 (vs $1.99), iPhone $57.58B and Mac $10.79B both records — yet shares fell ~3% after hours and −4.24% premarket on holiday-quarter gross-margin guidance of 45–46% vs 46.9% expected, with CEO John Ternus citing rising memory costs and slower services growth. If the market's calmest mega-cap, in its debut quarter under a new CEO, can't hold a beat, the grading curve for tonight's AMD print is brutal: beat-and-guide-clean, or sell. Zacks, Market Flux, Swingfolio
  2. The chip bounce is real but narrow (cautious, not bullish). SOXX +2.70% premarket after −3.79% Monday, with memory names leading (SanDisk +4.90%, Micron +3.56%) — the same memory complex whose rising prices just hurt Apple's margins. That tension is the trade: memory strength helps SOXL today and hurts Apple, so the Nasdaq-100 bounce (+0.34% futures) lags the chip bounce (+8.31% SOXL). A bounce led by one sector, against a falling mega-cap, is a retracement until proven otherwise. Swingfolio, Reuters via KWSN
  3. Election night is tonight's gap risk — and it is untradeable intraday (positioning day). All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are decided today; results land through the evening, prediction markets put Democratic Senate control at ~73–75%, and a Democratic House would mean a more gridlocked legislative backdrop. Nothing about the outcome is tradeable before the close — the session is for positioning small and being flat before results and AMD's print collide after the bell. Swingfolio, TipRanks
  4. Oil's whipsaw is the session's hidden volatility (watch, don't chase). Brent plunged ~8% Monday on diplomacy hopes, then rebounded ~1% on reports of limited US strikes on Qeshm Island — with officials simultaneously talking settlement. Energy names are bouncing (XOM +0.42%, CVX +0.15%) after Monday's −4% slides, but the headline round-trip in under 24 hours means oil can gap either way on a single headline; it is a reason to keep size modest, not a trade thesis. Swingfolio, Investopedia
  5. Shopify's −16% is the morning's single-stock warning (software stays punished). Shopify reported Q3 revenue $3.74B, in line with consensus, but guided Q4 gross-profit growth to the high twenties — and fell 16.18% premarket, alongside Palantir −5.91%, Salesforce −2.66% and Datadog −2.83%. Software is where beats go to die this week; keep TECL/TECS and any software-tilted long small. Swingfolio
