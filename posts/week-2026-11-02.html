5. Trading Implications (day-trading lens)

  1. Broad market — an election-and-payrolls reaction week (TQQQ/SQQQ, UPRO/SPXU). Tuesday's results and Friday's 8:30am jobs report are the two binary windows; everything else is positioning between them. TQQQ at $62.01 after a −10.5% Friday is damaged — the long side needs a reclaim, not a bounce guess, and SQQQ's +10.6% Friday leaves it extended enough that a soft election outcome or soft payrolls print could squeeze it hard. Trade the reaction to each event, never the event itself, and be flat into Friday's open.

  2. Semiconductors — the crowded trade just paid, which makes it dangerous (SOXL/SOXS). SOXS +25.0% Friday on SOXL −24.7% is the payoff every crowded short dreams of — and the reason Monday's open is a reversal-risk session, not a continuation chase. The week's chip catalysts are direct this time: AMD Tue AMC, Qualcomm/Arm Wed AMC. If AMD beats and holds, SOXL's Monday/Tuesday tape is a squeeze candidate off the $77 level; if AMD guides soft like Intel, SOXS continuation has a second leg. Size for a 20%+ daily range in either name — Friday proved that range is real.

  3. Small caps — the rotation vehicle, still intact (TNA/TZA). Russell +1.3% week, TNA +3.8% on the week despite Friday's −5.0%: the money leaving mega-cap tech is still landing here, and the 10-year below 5% (4.91–4.94%) is what lets it hold. TNA is the cleanest momentum long into Monday's ISM manufacturing print — but a hot Friday payrolls/wage print that re-prices December hikes is the thing that breaks the small-cap bid, so TZA is the flip on that trigger, not a hold-through.

  4. Apple Monday night is the week's first single-stock gap risk (TECL/TECS, TQQQ). Apple's ~$113B consensus quarter under a new CEO lands after the close Monday, with Palantir alongside. A soft Apple print would extend the mega-cap tech unwind into Election Day; a clean beat would give the Nasdaq its first genuine stabilization catalyst since Alphabet's fall. TECL is the direct expression, but TQQQ/SQQQ carry the same risk with far better liquidity — prefer them.

  5. Rates + oil — the two switches that override earnings. The 10-year at ~4.93% after a 35bp October rally is the cushion that kept Friday from being worse; a hot Friday wage print pushing it back toward 5% re-opens the tech drawdown. Brent at $117.64 (fourth straight monthly gain, +14–15% in October) is the live geopolitical tail — every session carries a Hormuz headline gap, and energy earnings are done, so the oil tape itself is now the only energy signal. Gold at a 52-week low (~$3,899) proves miners are not the hedge: don't chase NUGT on tech headlines.

House rules for the week: (a) ultra ETFs reset daily — intraday holds only, no overnight positions, and especially no carry into Monday's Apple close, Tuesday's election night, or Friday's jobs-report open; (b) Friday 8:30am ET is the week's peak event window — enter after the reaction, never before the print; (c) Fear (26) with retail at Extreme Fear (1) marks capitulation risk and short-covering risk — SOXS/SQQQ are extended trades, not safe ones; (d) inverse tails (TECS, FAZ, LABD, DUST) are tradeable but verify spreads live; (e) oil above $100 means every session carries a geopolitical gap tail — size for it, and be flat into the weekend.
